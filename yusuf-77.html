Tafsīr Reflection · Surah Yusuf, Āyah 77

A Pain Like No Other

قَالُوٓا۟ إِن يَسْرِقْ فَقَدْ سَرَقَ أَخٌۭ لَّهُۥ مِن قَبْلُ ۚ فَأَسَرَّهَا يُوسُفُ فِى نَفْسِهِۦ وَلَمْ يُبْدِهَا لَهُمْ ۚ قَالَ أَنتُمْ شَرٌّۭ مَّكَانًۭا ۖ وَٱللَّهُ أَعْلَمُ بِمَا تَصِفُونَ
"They said, 'If he steals — a brother of his has stolen before.' But Joseph kept it within himself and did not reveal it to them. He said, 'You are worse in position, and Allah is most knowing of what you describe.'" — Sūrah Yūsuf, 12:77

01

The Accusation Shifts

From "we are not thieves" to throwing him under the bus

  • Just moments earlier, the brothers had insisted, "Indeed, we are not thieves." Now they speak as if BinYamin — and "his brother" (Yusuf) — are separate from the rest of them.
    • They are clearly not speaking for everyone, only for themselves.
    • The implication is: "it's in their blood."
  • Part of the reasoning is that BinYamin and Yusuf share a different mother from the rest of the brothers.
  • Moral: this shows how family can be cruel to their own family.
  • There are things kept hidden that only family knows — but when even family calls someone a thief, others assume it must be true, believing that a family member wouldn't say it unless it were safe to say.
02

"It's From Your Dad's Side"

Inherited character — a Christian idea, not an Islamic one

Nature vs. Nurture
"This bad habit you have is from your dad's side."
  • Another dig folded into the accusation: bad character is treated as something passed down through bloodline.
  • This view resembles the Christian belief that humanity is sinful because a father figure's sin is inherited by all who come after him.
  • Islam takes a different view entirely: character is not a matter of bloodline inheritance — one person can be an idol-worshipper while his own son becomes someone like Ibrahim (AS).
03

The Narrations

Where "a brother of his has stolen before" comes from

Unverified origins
  • Several narrations attempt to explain this line, but they trace back to Sahaba who may have heard them from Jewish or Syriac traditions — meaning they are not considered authentic.
  • One narration in Muslim tradition holds that Yusuf's (AS) mother's father used to worship idols. She told young Yusuf (AS) to steal the idols so her father could no longer worship them — he was caught in the act, and that is why he was branded a thief.
  • A different narration from Syriac tradition instead says it was the mother herself who stole and was caught — making Yusuf "the son of a thief."
  • Also in the Syriac tradition, BinYamin was called "the brother of a liar" — because of the dreams he was said to have seen and described.
04

From the Qur'an Alone

The first generation didn't need outside stories

1st Generation Sahaba
  • The narrations above were passed down by the Tabi'un and Taba Tabi'in (the generations after the Sahaba) — but the first generation of Sahaba held no such views, since they relied on the Qur'an itself.
  • The likely explanation: the brothers simply seized an opportunity to further malign Yusuf (AS) in his absence — having already wronged him once, they threw in an accusation of theft as one more way to humiliate him without cause.
05

Present Tense, Present Cruelty

Why "if he steals" is worse than "he stole"

"In Yasriq"
  • The Arabic phrase used is "In yasriq" — "if he steals." "In" means "if," and the grammar of the clause that follows it matters.
  • An "if" clause paired with the past tense (e.g., "if I ate a burger") refers to a single, one-time occurrence.
  • An "if" clause paired with the present tense (e.g., "if I eat a burger [every day]") can carry the sense of a continuous, repeated action.
  • The brothers didn't say "he stole" — they chose the present tense, "if he steals," implying ongoing, habitual behavior rather than a single incident.
  • The unspoken accusation: "If he got caught this time, he's probably been stealing all along and usually gets away with it — no surprise, since it runs on the genetic side of that family."
  • This means they weren't only accusing BinYamin — they were insulting his mother and his entire lineage, without any real basis.
06

No Remorse, No Limits

Stating an allegation as if it were fact

  • The brothers show no remorse for what they had already done to Yusuf (AS) himself.
  • They present the allegation as though it were an established fact — a pattern some families fall into, speaking accusations with total certainty.
  • Earlier in the story, Yaqub (AS) had told Yusuf (AS) not to share his dream with his brothers, reminding him that Shaytan is an open enemy to mankind.
  • Shaytan's hatred of Adam (AS) drove him to get Adam and Hawwa expelled from Jannah, and to incite one of Adam's sons to kill the other — and his campaign against people has never stopped; he continues seizing every opportunity to lead people astray.
  • His deepest revenge against human beings is making them burn with the very fire he himself burns with — jealousy, hatred, and similar diseases of the heart.
Ibn al-Qayyim, commenting on Surah al-Falaq, taught that every disease has a cure — except for a few, for which the only remedy is to ask Allah for protection from them. Jealousy is one of those diseases.
07

The Silence That Screamed

"Fa-asarraha Yoosufu fee nafsihi" — a rage he refused to show

The hardest Sabr
  • At this moment, Yusuf (AS) kept it hidden inside himself and did not reveal it to them.
  • What exactly he hid is left undescribed — as if no single word could capture it.
  • He had the absolute power to crush them right there, yet he let none of it show; he kept that extreme rage locked inside.
  • The phrase "did not reveal it to them" seems redundant — keeping something secret already implies not revealing it. So why mention it twice?
  • Because concealing it was that difficult — like a volcanic eruption forced to stay contained.
  • Some people take pleasure in seeing others visibly hurt, even just a flicker of anger — Yusuf (AS) denied them even that.
  • Some scholars suggest it was sadness he suppressed rather than rage. It's also possible he had a ready answer for them but chose not to give it.
  • Yusuf (AS) had already been named earlier in the surah, so a pronoun alone would have sufficed here — yet the Qur'an names him again, signalling how serious this moment truly is; this was not easy to hold back.
This is the kind of ṣabr required when a person has the full power to take revenge — and still chooses restraint. This is the difficult kind of ṣabr. Patience combined with forgiveness and covering up wrongs is described in the Qur'an as one of the most remarkable things a person can achieve.
08

"You Are Worse in Position"

"Antum sharrun makana"

  • Yusuf (AS) finally responds, but only with this one line: "You are worse in position."
  • The implicit message: "You think he's in trouble for stealing? You are in far greater trouble than you could ever imagine."
  • Even if the accusation of theft against BinYamin were true, the brothers themselves are far more evil — they stole an actual child, and with him, a father's love.
  • Yusuf (AS) points out they are worse now than before: earlier, their wrongdoing at least had a motive — competing for their father's love. Now that motive is gone, and yet they continue insulting him regardless.
09

Allah Knows Best

"Wallahu a'lamu bima tasifoon"

Moral
  • Yusuf (AS) closes with: "Allah is most knowing of what you describe."
  • The stance here: he will not publicly declare that he knows better right now — but Allah knows.
  • After all these years, he still has no way to prove that he never stole.
There are accusations made against you by your family, and maybe by others, that you'll never be able to do anything about to clear your name. But Allah knows better.
10

Echoes of the Father

The same words, decades apart

Parallel with Verse 18
  • This exact phrase mirrors what Yaqub (AS) said decades earlier in Verse 18, when the brothers brought Yusuf's shirt stained with false blood: "And Allah is the one sought for help against that which you describe."
  • Here in Verse 77, Yusuf (AS) says essentially the same thing.
  • "Ma tasifoon" ("what [lies] you describe") first came from the father's mouth in response to his sons' lie — and decades later, the very same words come from the son's mouth in response to their lie once again.
  • As a child, Yusuf (AS) learned this language of describing liars from his father — and decades later, in a position of power, even his private thoughts mirror his father's words; he echoes his father without realizing it.
  • He remains connected to his father in spirit, even though they have been physically apart for years.
Moral

Family is a spiritual bond too

Family connection isn't only about love, providing food, or paying the bills — there is a spiritual thread that runs through it as well, one that can surface decades later without a person even realizing it.

Lesson

The imprint a parent leaves

Don't underestimate the value of simply talking to your kids, especially about Allah's Dīn. Islamic school and good videos help, but nothing compares to the imprint a parent personally leaves on a child's psyche — even a small childhood conversation can go on to shape how a person thinks decades later.

  • This single ayah shows two opposites within one family: the deepest wounds and scars family can inflict, alongside the most beautiful remnants of a good upbringing.
  • Even Yusuf's (AS) private thoughts, many years later, were shaped by the tarbiyah (upbringing) his father gave him long before.
