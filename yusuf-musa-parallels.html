Comparative Reflection · Sūrah Yūsuf & Sūrah Al‑Qaṣaṣ

Yūsuf & Mūsā (ʿalayhimā as‑salām)

Thirty‑two mirrored observations comparing the story of Prophet Yūsuf in Sūrah Yūsuf with the story of Prophet Mūsā in Sūrah Al‑Qaṣaṣ — where the two accounts run parallel, and where they run in opposite directions.

01

The Missing Parent

Each story begins by leaving one parent out

Points 1–2
Sūrah Yūsuf

Yūsuf's mother is never mentioned at all, and the story opens with an interaction between father and son.

Sūrah Al‑Qaṣaṣ

Mūsā's father is never mentioned at all, and the story opens with an interaction between mother and son.

02

Siblings' Role

Brothers cause separation; a sister causes reunion

Points 3–4
Sūrah Yūsuf

Yūsuf's own siblings are the reason he becomes separated from his family, having intellectually persuaded their father to let him go.

Sūrah Al‑Qaṣaṣ

Mūsā's sibling is the reason he is eventually reunited with his family; Allah, meanwhile, persuades Mūsā's mother emotionally to let him go.

03

The Unspoken Promise

A prophet left without assurance; a mother given one

Points 5–6
Sūrah Yūsuf

Yaʿqūb (A.S), a prophet, is never told whether he will ever be reunited with his son, and he weeps tears of sadness.

Sūrah Al‑Qaṣaṣ

Mūsā's mother, who is not a prophet, is told directly that she will be reunited with her son, and she weeps tears of happiness.

04

Waters & a New Home

Standing water, flowing water — and a reversed decision

Points 7–10
Sūrah Yūsuf

Yūsuf is thrown into a well — standing water — and is brought into his new household by a male figure, Egypt's minister. The minister tells his wife to take him either as a slave or as an adopted son; though the couple were childless, they choose to keep him as a slave.

Sūrah Al‑Qaṣaṣ

Mūsā is set adrift on a river — flowing water — and is brought into his new household by a female figure, Pharaoh's wife. She tells her husband to take him either as a slave or as an adopted son; though Mūsā was an Israelite, Pharaoh chooses to adopt him as a son.

05

Status, Gifts & Trials

Dignity and slavery trade places; each gift becomes a test

Points 11–14
Sūrah Yūsuf

Yūsuf's life moves from dignity to slavery. Allah grants him wisdom and knowledge, and also an exceptional trait — striking looks — which is exactly what draws him into trouble with the minister's wife.

Sūrah Al‑Qaṣaṣ

Mūsā's life moves from slavery to dignity. Allah grants him wisdom and knowledge, and also an exceptional trait — physical strength — which is exactly what draws him into trouble with the authorities.

06

The Crime

One crime prevented, one crime committed

Points 15–18
Sūrah Yūsuf

The near‑crime against Yūsuf is private in nature — an attempt at zinā, a killing of the soul. He seeks refuge in Allah before it happens, and — though innocent — ends up detained by the law.

Sūrah Al‑Qaṣaṣ

The crime involving Mūsā is public in nature — a killing of the body, a manslaughter. He seeks refuge in Allah after it happens, and — though guilty — ends up fleeing the law.

07

Trial, Flight & Help

Two men, two women — and how a man and woman should meet

Points 19–23
Sūrah Yūsuf

In jail, Yūsuf is approached by two men, and the story then continues with only one of them. This episode shows how a man and woman should not come together. An expected friend eventually secures his release from the trial of prison — but only after years — and does so for a self‑interested reason: he wants recognition from the king for interpreting his dream.

Sūrah Al‑Qaṣaṣ

While fleeing, Mūsā approaches two women, and the story then continues with only one of them. This episode shows how a man and woman should come together. An unexpected man saves him from the trial of being killed — but within days — and does so for a selfless reason: fear that Mūsā will be executed by the authorities.

08

Words & Actions

The power of speech versus the power of deeds

Points 24–25
Sūrah Yūsuf

Yūsuf helps his two cellmates through daʿwah — the power of words. He hopes for a favor in return from one of them, but it does not arrive right away.

Sūrah Al‑Qaṣaṣ

Mūsā helps the two women by watering their flock — the power of action. He does not expect any favor in return, yet one comes to him immediately.

09

Before the King

Both return to court; one is honored, one is humiliated

Points 26–28
Sūrah Yūsuf

Yūsuf returns to face the king. A confession takes place — the women who wronged him admit their fault — and Yūsuf is honored by the king.

Sūrah Al‑Qaṣaṣ

Mūsā returns to face the king. A confession also takes place — but this time it is Mūsā himself admitting his own fault — and Mūsā is humiliated by the king.

10

Reunion & Legacy

A silent brother, a shared sajdah, and Egypt entered and left

Points 29–32
Sūrah Yūsuf

Yūsuf is reunited with his brother Benjamin, who remains silent throughout. A sajdah occurs when Yūsuf's family prostrates after the reunion. Sūrah Yūsuf answers how the Israelites came to enter Egypt.

Sūrah Al‑Qaṣaṣ

Mūsā is reunited with his brother Hārūn, who also remains silent. A sajdah occurs when the magicians who lose to Mūsā fall in prostration. Sūrah Al‑Qaṣaṣ answers how the Israelites came to leave Egypt.

Two brothers, two journeys — one story closes with entry into Egypt, the other with exodus from it.
